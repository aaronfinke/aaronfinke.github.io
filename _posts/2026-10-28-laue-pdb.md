Raw mmCIF flag `_diffrn_radiation.pdbx_monochromatic_or_laue_m_l = 'L'` on an X-ray dataset: 172 rows — about a third are wrong, and ~20 genuine Laue entries carry `M` or nothing. The table below is the curated count.

## Method

1. Pulled every `diffrn_radiation` row with `m_l = 'L'`, `pdbx_diffrn_protocol` containing LAUE, or *laue / pink / polychromatic / white beam / broadband* in monochromator, source details/type or collimation (PDBj SQL). Kept `pdbx_scattering_type = 'x-ray'` and method `X-RAY DIFFRACTION`.
2. Added entries whose **title** says Laue / pink beam / polychromatic but whose flags say monochromatic (mostly 1990s entries and PAL 11C / BioCARS pink-beam SSX).
3. **Confirmed** = flagged Laue at a known polychromatic beamline (APS 14-ID-B / 14-BM-D BioCARS, ESRF ID09, NSLS X26C / X12C, SRS 9.7, CHESS, SSRF BL03HB, PAL 11C) **or** Laue/pink beam stated in title.
4. **Rejected** if flagged Laue but: Cu-Kα rotating anode at 1.54 Å, single wavelength at a monochromatic MX beamline, `M` + single λ, or the hit was a diamond Laue-*geometry* monochromator crystal (monochromatic beam).
5. **Ambiguous** = flagged Laue + a broad λ range (0.6–2.5 Å) quoted at monochromatic MX beamlines (probably the beamline tuning range copied in), XFEL SASE bandwidth 1.80–1.85 Å (LCLS CXI, 5CN4–5CNG), plus two ID09 time-resolved entries flagged `M` that literature suggests are Laue.
6. Year = initial release year (`pdbx_audit_revision_history`, first revision).

Observations:
- Two eras: 1990s–2000s classic time-resolved Laue (PYP, MbCO, IDH, Ras, AChE) at NSLS X26C, ESRF ID09, BioCARS; and 2017+ pink-beam serial crystallography (BioCARS, ID09, PAL 11C, SSRF BL03HB).
- BioCARS (APS 14-ID-B + 14-BM-D) dominates: 75 of 121 confirmed entries (~62%); ESRF ID09 next with 23.
- Recent spikes come from big time-resolved series (metallo-β-lactamase L1 2022, NaK2K EF-X 2023, DJ-1 mix-and-inject 2025).
- Early Laue structures (pre-~1996) are probably under-counted: many deposited without diffrn metadata and with no 'Laue' in the title.
