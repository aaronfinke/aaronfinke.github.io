I'm an instrument data scientist at the [European Spallation Source](https://www.ess.eu/) (ESS) in Kongens Lyngby, Denmark, part of Data Management and Scientific Computing (DMSC). My job is to make sure that when NMX, the neutron macromolecular crystallography instrument, produces its first data, there is software that can turn it into reliable structures. That means simulations of the instrument, time-of-flight Laue data reduction and integration (largely in [DIALS](https://dials.github.io/)), and working with the instrument team and future users.

## Background

I trained as a synthetic organic chemist (B.S. University of Arizona; Ph.D. University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign with Jeffrey Moore; postdoc at ETH Zürich with François Diederich), and moved into crystallography through small-molecule and materials structure work. A second postdoc at the Paul Scherrer Institute and Global Phasing focused on experimental phasing and data collection strategies at the Swiss Light Source. I then spent five years as a beamline scientist at MacCHESS (Cornell), building fixed-target serial and room-temperature crystallography capabilities, and two years at the BioMAX beamline at MAX IV. I have been working at the ESS since 2024.

## Interests

- Neutron and X-ray macromolecular crystallography
- Time-of-flight Laue data processing and instrument simulation
- Open-source scientific software
- Serial and room-temperature crystallography
